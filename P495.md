# Your task: v495, adaptive quality, UI, memory and load time (branch `perf-quality`)
Area: renderer settings (pixel ratio, resolution, shadow map size, antialias), a quality system and a Graphics setting, the HUD and DOM work each frame, memory over long runs, and load and startup time.

## Goals
1. **Adaptive quality.** A Graphics setting in the settings menu: Auto (default), High, Medium, Low.
   - **High** must look exactly like today.
   - **Medium and Low** may lower the render resolution scale, shadow map size or shadows on distant scenery, and particle density, in that order of preference.
   - **Auto** measures real frame times on the player's device and steps the quality down when frames are slow and back up when there is headroom. Use hysteresis (no flicker), and change resolution scale before anything visible.
   - Cap the device pixel ratio sensibly (phones report 3).
   - The setting persists in meta and is in the dev panel.
2. **HUD and DOM:** find per-frame DOM writes (`updHud`, `updSpells`, `updScrollBar`, `updPlaceUI`, `#vfxFlash` and `#vfxVig` style writes every frame, the menus), and write only when values change. No layout thrash.
3. **Memory over a long run:** play 30 waves in the bench harness and track `renderer.info.memory` (geometries, textures), the JS heap and scene children. Find and fix anything that grows wave over wave (geometries were 1055 to 1239 in one benchmark run).
4. **Load time:** about 3.2 s to ready headless. Profile the startup, and defer or lazily build heavy things nobody sees at once (Bestiary models, previews, far lands' assets), without hitches later.
5. **Test:** `tests/pql-test.js`, covering the tiers (High pixel-equal to today), Auto stepping down and up on simulated slow and fast frames without flicker, the setting persisting, the DOM writes only on change, no wave-over-wave growth, and load time not worse.
