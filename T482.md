# Your task: v482, a bespoke Armoured design for every troop that can be Armoured (branch `armour-troops`)
Simon: "armoured should not just be a template but rather a seperate design for every unit that can be armored".

## What exists
- **The templates:** `ELD.armored` calls `ARMOR_D[type](C)` if present, else `armorClass(C)`.
  - The class templates are one humanoid breastplate set, horse plates, siege plates, a bird plate and beast plates; the ogre has its own.
  - Wolves have no EBODY entry, so they get the soldier template, which is wrong.
- **Rules and stats:** knights and monks never roll Armoured (keep that). The modifier gives +0.25 armour.
- **Your units and their variants (`UNIT_VARIANTS`, `PEASANT_VARIANTS`):**
  - peasant: `PEASANT_VARIANTS`, several normal variants plus gem and scroll carriers;
  - soldier: Spearman, Swordsman, Axeman, Gem Carrier, Scroll Bearer;
  - rider: Scout, Lancer, Raider, Gem Courier, Scroll Courier (horse and rider);
  - raven: Raven, Hooded Crow, Blood Raven, Gem Thief, Scroll Thief;
  - ram: Timber Ram, Ram's Head, Spiked Ram, Gem Ram, Scroll Ram;
  - wolf: Taiga Wolf, from the Deep Taiga arena.
  The highlands arena has its own models for some units (search `Garron Scout`); cover those too.

## Build
1. **`ARMOR_D.peasant`, `.soldier`, `.rider`, `.raven`, `.ram` and `.wolf`.** Each is a design of its own that reads as "this unit, heavily armoured".
   - It uses the unit's own silhouette and style, is fitted to each variant (`C.variant`; measure the real meshes), and is attached to the parts that move: limbs, heads, wings, the horse's neck and legs, so nothing floats or clips while animating.
   - Examples:
     - Peasant: improvised armour, such as a pot helm, a padded jack with nailed scrap plates and a wooden shield strapped on.
     - Soldier: per variant, e.g. a brigandine and kettle hat for the Spearman, mail and a plated tabard for the Swordsman, lamellar for the Axeman.
     - Rider: a barded horse (chanfron, crinet, peytral, crupper) and an armoured rider.
     - Raven: a riveted beak cap, a feather-scale mail cape and talon guards.
     - Ram: iron-sheathed roof plates, a metal-clad head and riveted bands.
     - Wolf: a spiked collar, a chain-mail barding coat and a helm plate.
   - Use the house materials (`EM_METAL`, `EM_DARK`, `EM_RIVET`) or richer variants of them, flat shading to match the art, and some detail: rivets, straps, trims.
2. **Performance.** Build each design's geometry once per (type, variant) and cache it. Merge the static parts into few meshes, shared across foes. Only the parts that must follow a moving bone are separate.
3. **Tests:** `tests/arx-test.js`.
   - Each of your units and variants uses its own `ARMOR_D` design, not `armorClass`.
   - The pieces stay attached during animation: sample the world positions of armour pieces against their parent parts over a walk cycle.
   - Cover the caching (the same geometries are reused), cleanup, and no errors on every arena.
4. **Sheets:** each unit and variant, plain against armoured side by side, in the Bestiary and in game at zoom 1, plus close-ups.
